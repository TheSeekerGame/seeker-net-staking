Force-cancel all listings

Delisting on OpenSea is not enough: its quick cancel happens off-chain and leaves no public record, so your listing keeps counting until its end date. This button cancels it on-chain instead: one transaction, approved in your wallet, for your SEEKER NET listings only (nothing in other collections is touched). It costs a fraction of a cent, and your seekers earn again right away.

How it works, exactly

The start
Every wallet holding seekers at the start got 7 credits per seeker, capped at 100 per wallet, so every seeker has credits behind it from the very first sale. Nothing before the start counts.
Earning
Every unlisted seeker earns its owner 1 credit per day, counted by the second. Nothing to click or sign.
What "listed" means
A seeker is listed from the moment its holder lists it on OpenSea (any price, private listings too) until that listing's end date, unless the seeker is sold or moved first, or the listing is cancelled on-chain. Listed seekers earn nothing.
Ending a listing early
Delisting on OpenSea is not enough. OpenSea's quick cancel happens off-chain and leaves no public record, so the listing keeps counting until its end date. To stop it sooner, use "Force-cancel all listings" above, or pick a short duration when you list.
Selling and moving
When a seeker changes wallet (a sale, a gift, or a move between your own wallets), 7 credits go from the sender to the new owner, or all the sender has if fewer. Moves count in chain order: a wallet with 10 credits that sells two seekers gives 7, then 3. Flipping costs nothing: you get 7 when you buy and pass 7 on when you sell.
Where credits come from
Only from the start and from unlisted seekers. Moves just hand credits over. Credits can't be sent on their own and never go below zero.
Why it works this way
So anyone can check every number. Balances follow only from permanent public records, the chain and OpenSea's listing history, so anyone who replays them gets exactly the same result for every wallet, to the second. When the bot runs changes nothing. OpenSea's quick cancel leaves no such record, which is why it can't count.
Check it yourself
This page computes every balance in your browser from public files: the start, the moves, the on-chain cancellations and the listings, with one short rules file. The bot only copies these records, and its verify command rebuilds them from the chain and OpenSea to prove they match. Listings from the last hour can still be added as OpenSea's history catches up; older records are final. Nothing in the record is ever changed or removed; a correction, if ever needed, is added as its own entry with the reason.
